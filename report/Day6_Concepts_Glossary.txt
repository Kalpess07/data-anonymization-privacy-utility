1. INTRODUCTION

Data anonymization transforms selected attributes to reduce the risk of identifying individuals while retaining useful information for analysis. In this project, the uploaded adult_with_zip.csv dataset will be used to explore how selected transformations affect the data and, later, machine-learning utility. The specific quasi-identifiers and transformation rules must be agreed upon by the team.

2. KEY CONCEPTS

2.1 K-ANONYMITY
K-anonymity is a privacy model in which each record is indistinguishable from at least k-1 other records with respect to a selected set of quasi-identifiers. In practice, every combination of quasi-identifier values should occur in at least k records.

Dataset-related example:
If the team selects age and zip_code as quasi-identifiers, it could generalize age into ranges and mask or generalize zip_code, then count how many records share each resulting combination. For k=3, each combination must appear in at least three records to satisfy 3-anonymity for those chosen quasi-identifiers.

Important limitation:
This check depends on the selected quasi-identifiers and threat model. K-anonymity does not eliminate all privacy risks, and a group may still reveal a sensitive attribute if its members share the same value.

2.2 GENERALIZATION
Generalization replaces a detailed value with a broader category.

Example using age:
- Original age: 39
- Generalized age band: 30-39

The exact age bands should be selected consistently and documented. Generalization reduces detail and may affect analysis utility.

2.3 MASKING
Masking replaces part of a value with a symbol or less specific representation.

Example using zip_code:
- Original zip_code: 93810
- Masked example: 93***

This is an illustrative rule only. The team should confirm how many digits to retain and whether zip_code is an appropriate quasi-identifier for the study. Masking alone does not prove anonymity.

2.4 SUPPRESSION
Suppression hides or removes a selected value or record according to a defined rule.

Example using zip_code:
- Original zip_code: 93810
- Suppressed example: [SUPPRESSED]

The team should specify when suppression is applied, such as to selected values or records that do not meet a chosen privacy criterion. Suppression can reduce disclosure risk but may also remove useful information.

2.5 DIRECT IDENTIFIERS AND QUASI-IDENTIFIERS
A direct identifier directly identifies a person, such as a full name or a unique identification number. The inspected dataset does not contain an obvious name column. This does not mean the dataset is automatically anonymous.

Quasi-identifiers are attributes that may contribute to identification when combined with other information. Candidate fields for team review may include age, zip_code, sex, marital_status, relationship, workclass, and native_country. These are candidates for discussion, not a final classification.

4. SHORT GLOSSARY

Anonymization:
Transforming data to reduce the risk of identifying individuals. The protection achieved depends on the method and context.

Direct identifier:
A field that directly identifies a person, such as a name or unique ID.

Quasi-identifier:
A field that may help identify a person when combined with other fields or external information.

Sensitive attribute:
An attribute whose disclosure may reveal private information. The team should define which attributes are sensitive for this study.

Generalization:
Replacing a detailed value with a broader range or category.

Masking:
Concealing part of a value while leaving some characters visible.

Suppression:
Hiding or removing selected values or records under a defined rule.

K-anonymity:
A property in which each combination of selected quasi-identifier values is shared by at least k records.

Re-identification:
Linking records believed to be anonymous to an individual, often using external information.

Privacy-utility trade-off:
The balance between reducing privacy risk and retaining information useful for analysis or model performance.

Equivalence class:
A group of records sharing the same values for the selected quasi-identifiers after transformation.
