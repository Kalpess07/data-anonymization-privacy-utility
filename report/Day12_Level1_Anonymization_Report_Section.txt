1. Level 1 Anonymization Methodology

Level 1 applies basic transformations intended to reduce the precision of selected attributes while retaining useful information for analysis. The project’s Level 1 examples describe age generalization into ten-year ranges and masking the final two characters of valid five-digit ZIP-like values. The before/after examples below are illustrative and should be checked against executed notebook output before being described as verified row-level results.

2. Direct Identifier Removal

Direct identifiers, such as a person's name, should be removed before releasing an anonymized dataset when such fields are present and are not required for analysis. However, the listed columns for the Adult dataset do not show an obvious name field. The available evidence therefore does not establish that a name column was removed in this specific dataset. State name removal as a general policy unless the team confirms that a name-like field existed and was removed.

3. Age Bucketing (Generalization)

Valid age values are mapped to ten-year intervals. This reduces exact age precision while retaining approximate age-group information. Examples:
- 23 becomes 20-29
- 37 becomes 30-39
- 61 becomes 60-69

The exact boundary and missing-value behavior should follow the implementation in src/level1_encoding.py.

4. ZIP-Code Masking

For valid five-digit values, the Level 1 example rule retains the first three characters and masks the final two characters:
- 93810 becomes 938**
- 24592 becomes 245**

The source table describes missing values as remaining missing, but the code's behavior for invalid or missing values should be confirmed against executed output. Masking reduces visible detail; it does not by itself establish that a value cannot be linked to other information.

7. Privacy Considerations and Limitations

Age generalization and ZIP masking reduce the precision of individual attributes but do not automatically guarantee k-anonymity or prevent re-identification. Risk depends on the combination of quasi-identifiers, the distribution of records, external information available to an attacker, and the threat model. Name the quasi-identifiers used in the privacy evaluation and include measured k-anonymity or suppression results only when those metrics have actually been calculated.
